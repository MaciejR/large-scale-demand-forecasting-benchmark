\section{Sensitivity Analyses}\label{app:sensitivity}

Five sensitivity variants of the primary $k = 543$ model-level
meta-regression are reported below.  All models use
\texttt{metafor::rma.mv} \citep{viechtbauer2010metafor} with REML
estimation, Knapp--Hartung $t$-distribution adjustment
\citep{knapp2003improved}, and nesting
\texttt{\~{}1\,|\,dataset\_norm/paper\_id}.

\subsection{Interpretation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Excl-M5 ($k = 468$):} Excluding all M5 pairs barely
    changes the estimate ($-0.183$ vs $-0.195$) and remains
    highly significant ($p = 0.0002$).  M5 is not driving the primary
    result.

  \item \textbf{\WAPE{}-only ($k = 211$):} The FM advantage is
    statistically significant ($\hat{\Delta} = -0.116$, $p = 0.018$),
    but the effect is smaller than the SQL-only finding, confirming the
    metric gradient documented in \S\ref{subsec:metric-moderator}.

  \item \textbf{SQL-only ($k = 166$):} The strongest signal in the
    pool: FMs beat baselines by 0.318 SQL units ($p < .0001$), with
    the lowest $Q$ of any slice, indicating relative homogeneity
    conditional on this metric.

  \item \textbf{Source~B only ($k = 45$):} The 45 within-paper pairs
    (5~FMs $\times$ 9~cells) show the right direction ($-0.251$) but
    are insufficient for significance ($p = 0.16$) with only 3~dataset
    clusters.

  \item \textbf{Source~A only ($k = 498$):} Highly significant
    ($p < .0001$).  The cross-benchmark pairs from fev-bench and
    GIFT-Eval carry most of the statistical mass.
\end{enumerate}

$k = 10$:

The model-level design ($k = 543$) differs from the bucket-level
variant in two ways: (1)~it preserves within-task heterogeneity
instead of averaging it away, and (2)~it includes fev-bench and
GIFT-Eval multi-metric data (SQL, MASE, WAPE), which contributes
substantial statistical mass.  Together, these shift the conclusion
from ``null, heterogeneous'' to ``significantly better on both
distributional and point-forecast metrics.''
