\section{Sensitivity Analyses}\label{app:sensitivity}

Three variants of the meta-regression are reported to assess
robustness of the headline finding (\S6.7). All models use
\texttt{metafor::rma.mv} \citep{viechtbauer2010metafor} with REML
estimation and Knapp--Hartung $t$-distribution adjustment
\citep{knapp2003improved}.

\subsection{Full Cross-Paper Pool ($k = 10$)}

Model: \texttt{rma.mv(yi = delta, V = vi, random = \~{}1|dataset\_norm,
test = "t", method = "REML")} on the 10 cells of Table~6.1.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Full cross-paper pool ($k = 10$).}\label{tab:sensitivity-full}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Parameter & Value \\
\midrule
$k$                    & 10 \\
Intercept ($\Dhat$)    & $+0.101$ \\
SE                     & 0.145 \\
$t(9)$                 & 0.694 \\
$p$-value              & 0.505 \\
95\% CI                & $[-0.227, +0.429]$ \\
$\sigma^2$ (dataset)   & 0.063 \\
$Q(9)$                 & 1055.3, $p < .0001$ \\
$I^2$                  & 99.1\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Interpretation.} The full-pool intercept is positive
(FM worse on average) but far from significant. The extreme
heterogeneity ($I^2 = 99.1\%$) is driven by the three M5 \WAPE{}
cells ($\Delta = -0.54$ to $-0.78$), which are artefactual (\S5.4.5),
and the M5 \WRMSSE{} cell ($\Delta = +0.41$), which goes in the
opposite direction.

\subsection{Excluding M5 \WAPE{} ($k = 6$)}

Same model specification, dropping the three M5 \WAPE{} cells
but retaining M5 \WRMSSE{}.

\textbf{Note:} The $k = 6$ pool in this table excludes only the three
M5 \WAPE{} cells. The M5 \WRMSSE{} cell ($k = 1$) is retained because
\WRMSSE{} does not suffer from the per-series denominator
pathology. However, because the M5 \WRMSSE{} cell is the only
non-\WAPE{} cell in the pool, the excl-M5 intercept is reported
as the primary result to avoid conflating metric effects.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Excluding M5 \WAPE{} ($k = 6$).}\label{tab:sensitivity-exclm5}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Parameter & Value \\
\midrule
$k$                    & 6 \\
Intercept ($\Dhat$)    & $-0.044$ \\
SE                     & 0.028 \\
$t(5)$                 & $-1.583$ \\
$p$-value              & 0.174 \\
95\% CI                & $[-0.115, +0.027]$ \\
$\sigma^2$ (dataset)   & 0.001 \\
$Q(5)$                 & 2.00, $p = 0.849$ \\
$I^2$                  & 0.0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Interpretation.} After removing the M5 \WAPE{} artefact, the
intercept is small and negative (FM slightly better) but not
significant. Heterogeneity drops to zero: the six remaining
cells (Favorita $\times$ 3 + Rohlik $\times$ 3) are homogeneous. This is
the primary result reported in the Abstract and \S9.

\subsection{Within-Paper Only --- Source B ($k = 9$)}

Model: \texttt{rma.mv(yi = delta, V = vi, random = \~{}1|paper\_id +
\~{}1|paper\_id/dataset\_norm, test = "t", method = "REML")} on the
9 Source~B paired cells (3 datasets $\times$ 3 horizons, paper\_id =
LOCAL\_MAC\_*). Variance proxy: $v_i = 1/n_\text{valid} = 0.01$.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Within-paper only --- Source~B ($k = 9$).}\label{tab:sensitivity-within}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Parameter & Value \\
\midrule
$k$                          & 9 \\
Intercept ($\Dhat$)          & $-0.245$ \\
SE                           & 0.103 \\
$t(8)$                       & $-2.386$ \\
$p$-value                    & 0.044 \\
95\% CI                      & $[-0.482, -0.008]$ \\
$\sigma^2_1$ (paper\_id)     & 0.043 \\
$\sigma^2_2$ (paper/dataset) & 0.043 \\
$Q(8)$                       & 76.00, $p < .0001$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\textbf{Interpretation.} The within-paper model reaches significance
($p = 0.044$) with a substantial negative intercept (FM better
by $\sim$25 pp on average across all 9 cells). However, this result
is dominated by the three M5 cells ($\Delta = -0.54$ to $-0.78$). After
mentally excluding M5, the Favorita and Rohlik cells contribute
$\Delta \in [-0.01, -0.09]$, consistent with the cross-paper excl-M5
finding. The within-paper model is not used as the primary
result because (i) it has only one paper\_id (no between-study
variation) and (ii) the M5 \WAPE{} artefact inflates the intercept.

\subsection{Summary Across Sensitivity Variants}

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Summary of sensitivity variants.}\label{tab:sensitivity-summary}
\begin{tabular}{lrlll}
\toprule
Variant & $k$ & $\Dhat$ & 95\% CI & $p$ \\
\midrule
Full pool        & 10 & $+0.101$ & $[-0.227, +0.429]$ & 0.505 \\
Excl-M5 \WAPE{}  & 6  & $-0.044$ & $[-0.115, +0.027]$ & 0.174 \\
Within-paper (B) & 9  & $-0.245$ & $[-0.482, -0.008]$ & 0.044 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The three variants tell a consistent story: the apparent FM
advantage in the full pool is a composition effect driven by the
M5 \WAPE{} artefact. Once M5 \WAPE{} is excluded, the FM-vs-\LGBM{}
gap is small ($-0.044$ \WAPE{}), not significant ($p = 0.174$), and
homogeneous ($I^2 = 0\%$).
