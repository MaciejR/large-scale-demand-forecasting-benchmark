\section{Sensitivity Analyses}\label{app:sensitivity}

Five sensitivity variants of the primary $k = 420$ model-level
meta-regression are reported below.  All models use
\texttt{metafor::rma.mv} \citep{viechtbauer2010metafor} with REML
estimation, Knapp--Hartung $t$-distribution adjustment
\citep{knapp2003improved}, and nesting
\texttt{\~{}1\,|\,dataset\_norm/paper\_id}.

\subsection{Interpretation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Excl-M5 ($k = 363$):} Excluding all M5 pairs barely
    changes the estimate ($-0.126$ vs $-0.132$) and remains significant
    ($p = 0.026$).  M5 is not driving the primary result, unlike in the
    original $k = 10$ analysis.

  \item \textbf{\WAPE{}-only ($k = 164$):} The FM advantage
    \emph{vanishes} on this point-forecast metric ($\hat{\Delta} =
    +0.097$, $p = 0.30$).  This is the mirror of the SQL-only finding
    and confirms that the metric split documented in
    \S\ref{subsec:metric-moderator} is robust.

  \item \textbf{SQL-only ($k = 128$):} The strongest signal in the
    pool: FMs beat baselines by 0.345 SQL units ($p < .0001$), with
    the lowest $Q$ of any slice, indicating relative homogeneity
    conditional on this metric.

  \item \textbf{Source~B only ($k = 36$):} The 36 within-paper pairs
    (4~FMs $\times$ 9~cells) show the right direction ($-0.229$) but
    are insufficient for significance ($p = 0.18$) with only 3~dataset
    clusters.

  \item \textbf{Source~A only ($k = 384$):} Marginal significance
    ($p = 0.063$).  The cross-benchmark pairs from fev-bench and
    GIFT-Eval carry most of the statistical mass.
\end{enumerate}

The expansion from $k = 10$ to $k = 420$ (via fev-bench and GIFT-Eval
re-extraction and Source~B expansion from 2 to 4~FMs) changes the
primary conclusion from ``null, heterogeneous'' to ``significantly
better on average, metric-dependent.''  The key driver is the
fev-bench SQL metric, which was not represented in the original
extraction.
