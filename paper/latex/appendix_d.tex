\section{Sensitivity Analyses}\label{app:sensitivity}

Five sensitivity variants of the primary $k = 450$ model-level
meta-regression are reported below.  All models use
\texttt{metafor::rma.mv} \citep{viechtbauer2010metafor} with REML
estimation, Knapp--Hartung $t$-distribution adjustment
\citep{knapp2003improved}, and nesting
\texttt{\~{}1\,|\,dataset\_norm/paper\_id}.

\subsection{Interpretation}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Excl-M5 ($k = 378$):} Excluding all M5 pairs barely
    changes the estimate ($-0.116$ vs $-0.150$) and remains
    significant ($p = 0.004$).  M5 is not driving the primary result
    (contrast with the bucket-level variant in Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity-old},
    where excluding M5 shifted the estimate substantially).

  \item \textbf{\WAPE{}-only ($k = 174$):} The FM advantage is
    directionally favorable but not statistically significant
    ($\hat{\Delta} = -0.070$, $p = 0.14$).  The effect is weaker than
    the SQL-only finding, confirming that the metric split documented in
    \S\ref{subsec:metric-moderator} is robust.

  \item \textbf{SQL-only ($k = 138$):} The strongest signal in the
    pool: FMs beat baselines by 0.278 SQL units ($p < .0001$), with
    the lowest $Q$ of any slice, indicating relative homogeneity
    conditional on this metric.

  \item \textbf{Source~B only ($k = 36$):} The 36 within-paper pairs
    (4~FMs $\times$ 9~cells) show the right direction ($-0.229$) but
    are insufficient for significance ($p = 0.18$) with only 3~dataset
    clusters.

  \item \textbf{Source~A only ($k = 414$):} Highly significant
    ($p < .0001$).  The cross-benchmark pairs from fev-bench and
    GIFT-Eval carry most of the statistical mass.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Bucket-Level Variant ($k = 10$)}

For comparison, a coarser bucket-level aggregation
(mean FM vs mean ML\_TREE per dataset--horizon--metric cell) yields
$k = 10$:

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Bucket-level variant ($k = 10$),
  retained for comparison.}\label{tab:sensitivity-old}
\begin{tabular}{lrlll}
\toprule
Variant & $k$ & $\Dhat$ & 95\% CI & $p$ \\
\midrule
Full pool        & 10 & $+0.101$ & $[-0.227, +0.429]$ & 0.505 \\
Excl-M5 \WAPE{}  & 6  & $-0.044$ & $[-0.115, +0.027]$ & 0.174 \\
Within-paper (B) & 9  & $-0.245$ & $[-0.482, -0.008]$ & 0.044 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The model-level design ($k = 450$) differs from the bucket-level
variant in two ways: (1)~it preserves within-task heterogeneity
instead of averaging it away, and (2)~it includes the fev-bench SQL
metric, which contributes substantial statistical mass.  Together,
these shift the conclusion from ``null, heterogeneous'' to
``significantly better on average, metric-dependent.''
