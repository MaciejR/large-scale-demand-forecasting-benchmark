\begin{abstract}
We examine when zero-shot time-series foundation models improve short-term
retail demand forecasts over pragmatic LightGBM and statistical baselines.
On matched 100-series rolling-origin panels from M5, Rohlik~v2, and Favorita,
five foundation models and seven baseline variants are compared using paired
series-bootstrap covariance.  Chronos-2 achieves lower aggregate \WAPE{} than
the best observed local baseline in all nine dataset--horizon cells;
richer-feature and 200-trial LightGBM sensitivity checks do not reverse the M5
result.  Separate prediction-level reruns of 20 official fev-bench retail tasks
provide paired \WAPE{} covariance against Seasonal Naive.  Public fev-bench and
GIFT-Eval results are used descriptively: foundation-model advantages are
clearest for Scaled Quantile Loss and smaller for \WAPE{} and \MASE{}.
Rankings remain conditional on metric, baseline strength, multi-step protocol,
workload matching, leakage risk, and demand characteristics.  The released
artifacts support auditable retail forecasting bake-offs.
\end{abstract}
