\author[inst1]{Maciej Rubczynski\corref{cor1}}
\ead{rubczynski.maciej@gmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}

\begin{abstract}
We study when zero-shot time-series foundation models improve short-term
retail demand forecasts relative to pragmatic LightGBM and statistical
baselines.  The core evidence is a matched-panel Source~B experiment on M5,
Rohlik~v2, and Favorita: five zero-shot foundation models and six baseline
variants are evaluated on the same 100-series rolling-origin panels, with
paired series-bootstrap log-ratio covariance.  Chronos-2 has lower aggregate
\WAPE{} than the best observed local baseline in all nine dataset--horizon
cells; TiRex is lower in all nine point estimates and Moirai~2.0-Small in
eight of nine; both have intervals excluding zero in seven cells.  On M5, a
stratified intermittent panel
preserves the Chronos-2 advantage against the 10-trial tuned \LGBM{} baseline,
and a 200-trial \LGBM{} stress test leaves Chronos-2 below the best observed
high-budget \LGBM{} envelope within the fixed feature set at all three
horizons ($\ell=-0.063,-0.073,-0.061$).  The local pattern includes
Rohlik~v2, for which the evaluated model papers provide no documented
pretraining exposure, although this is not a leakage audit.  A separate
prediction-level rerun of
official fev-bench retail windows provides paired \WAPE{} covariance matrices
for 20 tasks.  Public fev-bench and GIFT-Eval extraction is used descriptively:
SQL shows the clearest directional FM advantage (median log-ratio $-0.229$;
FM lower in 92\% of rows), whereas \WAPE{}/ND (median $-0.047$; 66\%) and
\MASE{} (median $-0.036$; 61\%) are closer to conventional baselines.  The
practical conclusion is conditional: rankings depend on metric, baseline
feature set, multi-step protocol, workload matching, leakage risk, and demand
characteristics.  We release prediction-level artefacts, covariance matrices,
and a validation checklist for retail bake-offs.
\end{abstract}

\href{https://github.com/MaciejR/large-scale-demand-forecasting-benchmark}{GitHub};
the release package corresponding to this manuscript is prepared as a
versioned Zenodo-ready archive under \path{release/} rather than overwriting the
historical snapshot, and is prepared as GitHub release v1.18 at
\href{https://github.com/MaciejR/large-scale-demand-forecasting-benchmark/releases/tag/v1.18}{the v1.18 release}.

tables/figures, study-characteristics data, audit outputs, and replication
scripts, but not raw competition data or untracked fev-bench prediction
parquet trees.

dataset is publicly available via Kaggle; the Rohlik~v2 dataset
is available via the
\href{https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rohlik-sales-forecasting-challenge-v2}{Rohlik~v2 Kaggle competition}.
