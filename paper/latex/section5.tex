% -----------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}\label{sec:experimental-setup}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------

All gap-filling experiments described in \S\ref{sec:experiments} follow a single
experimental protocol, parameterized per dataset.  This section
defines the protocol once.  Dataset-specific choices (covariate
sets, eval-window lengths, series-count caps) are noted inline in
\S\ref{sec:results} for baseline models and \S\ref{sec:fm} for
foundation models.

\subsubsection{Datasets}\label{sec:datasets}

We evaluate on three retail demand forecasting datasets, chosen
to span the distribution of zero-day fractions in public retail
benchmarks while keeping the hierarchical-SKU $\times$ location
structure that retail practitioners actually face.  The three
datasets together cover ${\sim}211\text{k} \times 1.4\text{k}$
series-days across the full product $\times$ location $\times$
time tensor.

Three notes on dataset preparation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{M5} is used in full.  The 30{,}490 store-item series
  correspond to the Walmart subset of the M5 competition
  \citep{makridakis2022m5}; we use the official tail 389 days as the
  evaluation window (\S\ref{sec:evalprotocol}).

\item \textbf{Rohlik v2} is used in full.  The dataset is 5{,}390
  warehouse-SKU series from the Kaggle competition
  \texttt{rohlik-sales-forecasting-challenge-v2}, covering
  2020-08-01 to 2024-06-02 across 7 warehouses (Prague $\times$3,
  Brno, Munich, Frankfurt, Budapest).  Approximately 31\% of Rohlik
  SKUs enter later than day~1 (ragged series starts), which we
  handle at the wide-pivot step via \texttt{fillna(0)} --- see
  \S\ref{sec:modelfamilies}.

\item \textbf{Favorita} is capped at the top 30{,}000 series by
  total \texttt{unit\_sales} across the training portion, matching
  M5's scale for cross-dataset forecast-count comparability.  The
  full dataset has ${\sim}174{,}685$ series at ${\sim}125$\,M rows
  and does not fit the 32\,GB E4DS\_V4 compute used for Phases
  A--D.  The selection rule biases Favorita \LGBM{} evaluation
  toward higher-velocity series; this is flagged as a limitation
  in \S\ref{sec:threats} and \S\ref{sec:framework}, and the full-series evaluation
  is left for future work on larger hardware.
\end{itemize}

All three datasets are registered as versioned Azure ML data
assets (\texttt{azureml:m5-sales:1}, \texttt{azureml:rohlik-train-v2:1},
\texttt{azureml:favorita-train:1}, plus per-dataset covariate
assets) for exact reproducibility.

\subsubsection{Compute and Tracking}\label{sec:compute}

All jobs run on Azure ML compute cluster \texttt{cc-forecast-batch}
(E4DS\_V4 VM, 4 vCPU / 32\,GB RAM / no GPU) in region
\texttt{swedencentral}, inside Docker environment
\texttt{forecast-benchmark-cpu-env:1}.  We use
\texttt{cc-forecast-batch} for the classical and ML baselines of
\S\ref{sec:results}--\S\ref{sec:baseline}.  Foundation model
sweeps (\S\ref{sec:fm}) run on consumer hardware (Apple M-series
MPS); setup details are in \S\ref{sec:fm_scope}.

Experiment orchestration uses Azure ML pipelines defined in
\texttt{benchmark/code/pipelines/\{m5,rohlik,favorita\}\_consolidated.yaml}.
Every run logs metrics, parameters, runtime, and per-series CSVs
via MLflow to the workspace tracking server.  Cost and CO$_2$
estimates are computed inside the job using Azure's published
per-second pricing for \texttt{Standard\_E4ds\_v4} in
\texttt{swedencentral} (\$0.38/hr at time of writing) and the
region's marginal CO$_2$ intensity (0.041\,kgCO$_2$eq/kWh,
swedencentral 2024 baseline; Nowtricity/Ember).  The per-job runtime, cost, and
CO$_2$ columns in Tables~\ref{tab:t53}--\ref{tab:t55} are
computed this way and are therefore comparable across datasets.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:evalprotocol}

For each dataset we use \textbf{rolling-origin non-overlapping
evaluation with a held-out tail window}, which is the protocol
most commonly used in the FM papers we screened (\S\ref{sec:prisma-results}) and which
gives a clean, reviewer-auditable split.  Specifically:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Train / eval split:}
  $\texttt{train\_until} = \lfloor 0.8 \times n_\text{days} \rfloor$.
  This gives a tail-eval window of 389 days on M5, 281 days on
  Rohlik, and 337 days on Favorita.  The choice of 0.8 follows
  \citet{hewamalage2023forecast} and
  \citet{shchur2025fevbench}.

\item \textbf{Rolling origin:} within the tail window, forecast
  origins are spaced exactly $h$ days apart so that windows are
  non-overlapping.  For example at $h = 14$ on Rohlik (eval
  window $= 281$ days), we have $\lfloor 281/14 \rfloor = 20$
  evaluation windows per series.  This keeps per-horizon sample
  counts independent of each other and avoids the optimistic bias
  of overlapping-windows evaluation.

\item \textbf{Horizons:} $h \in \{7, 14, 28\}$ on all three
  datasets.  Seven days is the operational horizon most retailers
  use for short-term replenishment; 28 days is the M5 competition
  horizon and a common upper bound in the literature
  \citep{makridakis2022m5}; 14 days bridges the two.  We report
  every metric at every horizon to enable horizon-conditioned
  interpretation.

\item \textbf{Metrics:} MAE, sMAPE, \WAPE{}, and (on M5 only)
  \WRMSSE{}.  MAE and \WAPE{} are computed per series and
  averaged with equal weights (\texttt{\_mean} suffix in the
  tables).  sMAPE uses the classical symmetric form with a 200\%
  cap on zero actuals.  \WRMSSE{} is computed in-job via the full
  12-level \citet{makridakis2022m5} hierarchy from the raw
  sales/calendar/prices CSVs.  On retail tasks with heavy right
  tails (all three of ours), sMAPE is reported only for
  reproducibility and is not used in any primary comparison ---
  see \S\ref{sec:takeaways} for the cross-dataset justification.

\item \textbf{Per-series filtering for \WAPE{}:} \WAPE{} requires
  a non-zero denominator (sum of actuals in the eval window).
  For series where the denominator is zero we drop the series from
  \WAPE{} computation and report the \texttt{n\_valid} count
  alongside the metric in Tables~\ref{tab:t53}--\ref{tab:t55}.
  This affects at most 0.5\,\% of M5 series and a higher fraction
  on Rohlik (where ragged starts leave some series with only a few
  eval-window days); on Favorita the fraction is negligible for
  \LGBM{} and ${\sim}50\,\%$ for SN, an \texttt{n\_valid}
  asymmetry we discuss in \S\ref{sec:favorita}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Source~B sampling audit and repair.}  The initial Source~B plan
was to evaluate each FM on a fixed 100-series sample per cell and pair it
against baselines on the same sampled workload.  The integrity audit showed
that the saved 72-row result file did not satisfy that condition for all
models.  The legacy sweep is therefore retained only as provenance and
cost-audit material.  The matched-panel repair run covers
M5, Rohlik, and Favorita: the runner
\texttt{benchmark/code/experiments/run\_source\_b\_paired\_panel.py}
samples the same 100 top-volume series per dataset, stores the series
manifest, writes prediction-level Parquet files, and computes paired
bootstrap log-ratio contrasts against the best available baseline in the
same dataset--horizon cell.  The completed matched panel covers
Seasonal Naive, recursive \LGBM{}, direct \LGBM{}, horizon-scaled direct
\LGBM{}, Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Chronos-2, Moirai~2.0-Small, TimesFM~2.5,
and batched TiRex for M5/Rohlik/Favorita at $h\in\{7,14,28\}$.
Audit outputs are written to
\texttt{analysis/figures/source\_b\_paired\_panel\_*}.

\subsubsection{Model Families}\label{sec:modelfamilies}

Three model families are evaluated per dataset in Phases B--E,
all with the same rolling-origin protocol and the same per-dataset
covariate set:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Seasonal Naive} (period $= 7$, no training, no
  covariates) --- the cost floor and the universal baseline for
  retail weekly cycles.

\item \textbf{LightGBM recursive} --- one global Tweedie model per
  dataset, fit with \texttt{variance\_power = 1.1}, 300 boosting
  rounds, \texttt{num\_leaves = 63}, minimum child samples $= 20$,
  learning rate 0.05, \texttt{feature\_fraction = 0.8},
  \texttt{bagging\_fraction = 0.8}.  The model uses lags
  $\{1, 7, 14\}$, rolling means $\{7, 14\}$, and the
  dataset-specific covariates listed in Table~\ref{tab:t51}.  At
  evaluation time, the model is applied recursively: predictions
  at step $k$ enter the lag features for step $k + 1$, and
  future-known covariates are passed through unchanged.

\item \textbf{LightGBM direct} --- $h$ separate global Tweedie
  models per dataset, same hyperparameters and same feature set as
  the recursive variant, but model $k$ predicts $y[t + k]$ from
  lags and rolling means known at $t$ plus future-known covariates
  at $t + k$.  No output feedback, no recursion.
\end{itemize}

The direct-vs-recursive contrast with everything else held
constant is the core protocol comparison of \S\ref{sec:baseline}.
Hyperparameters are intentionally untuned beyond library defaults:
the point of \S\ref{sec:baseline} is to characterize the
\emph{protocol gap} between direct and recursive variants of an
otherwise-fixed LightGBM, not to reach the M5 winner.  Ceiling
claims for tuned LightGBM require additional experiments and are treated
as future work in \S\ref{sec:threats}.

For each training window we use the \textbf{last 365 days of the
training portion} as the training set.  This cap is a deliberate
scalability choice --- fitting ${\sim}10$--$30$\,M rows per
LightGBM model takes 180--240\,s on E4DS\_V4 --- and we estimate
in \S\ref{sec:threats} that extending to the full training
portion may improve \WRMSSE{} on M5.  The
same cap applies on Rohlik and Favorita for comparability.

Foundation models are evaluated zero-shot with each model's published
inference script on consumer hardware (Apple M-series MPS/CPU).  The
legacy Source~B sweep covers Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Moirai~2.0-Small, TiRex,
Chronos-2, and TimesFM~2.5, but not on a common workload.  The
matched-panel repair covers Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Chronos-2,
Moirai~2.0-Small, TiRex, and TimesFM~2.5 on M5/Rohlik/Favorita,
with shared series IDs, shared forecast origins, and paired
prediction outputs.  Details of the FM protocol,
context-length choices, and hardware configuration are in \S\ref{sec:fm}.

\subsubsection{Reproducibility}\label{sec:reproducibility}

Every result is tied to a git commit SHA, Azure ML pipeline run
name, registered data asset version, and a versioned environment

\subsection{Baseline Reliability: What Is a Fair LightGBM on
Retail Demand Forecasting?}\label{sec:baseline}\label{sec:baseline-reliability}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------

Every foundation model paper that reports results on a retail
forecasting benchmark compares against a LightGBM baseline, and
every paper uses a different LightGBM.  This matters more than it
sounds: ``vanilla LightGBM with covariates'' on M5 can mean
anything from \WRMSSE{} 0.70 to \WRMSSE{} 0.56 depending on two
protocol choices that are rarely stated --- \textbf{how the
training window is selected} and \textbf{whether multi-step
prediction is recursive or direct}.  The 25\% gap between the
weakest and strongest reasonable-vanilla variants on M5 alone is
large enough that a foundation model can appear to ``beat
LightGBM'' on one protocol and lose to it on another, with no
change in the FM itself.

The \textbf{direction} of the protocol gap is not constant
across datasets or metric definitions.  In the legacy full-workload
per-series \WAPE{} audit, direct LightGBM is much better than recursive
LightGBM on M5, while recursive is better on Rohlik and Favorita under
the fixed-budget runs.  In the matched 100-series aggregate-\WAPE{}
panel, however, the M5 gap is small at $h=7$ and $h=14$ and direct is
only lower at $h=28$; Favorita shows the same mixed pattern, with
recursive lower at $h=7$ and $h=14$ and direct lower at $h=28$.  The
``direct dominates recursive'' pattern is
therefore conditional on dataset, workload, metric aggregation, and
compute budget, not a property of multi-step LightGBM protocol choice in
the abstract.  Intermittency is a plausible moderator, but the current
three-dataset evidence cannot identify it as a causal boundary.  The
established
direct-vs-recursive literature \citep{bentaieb2014boosting,
bentaieb2016bias} conditions the choice on model linearity and
horizon length but not on demand intermittency; our finding that
the gap sign varies across these retail datasets is therefore a
hypothesis requiring further investigation.  \S\ref{sec:synthesis}
develops this synthesis
across all three datasets.

We instantiate this range with the three-variant sweep of
\S\ref{sec:modelfamilies} (Seasonal Naive, LightGBM recursive,
LightGBM direct) on the held-out M5 tail of
\S\ref{sec:evalprotocol} (days 1552--1940, 389 evaluation days).
Model families, training window, hyperparameters, feature set, and
rolling-origin protocol are all defined once in
\S\ref{sec:setup} and not repeated here.  The rest of
\S\ref{sec:baseline} analyses the nine-row table these settings
produce and extends the analysis to Rohlik~v2
(\S\ref{sec:rohlik}) and Favorita (\S\ref{sec:favorita}) with the
same protocol.

The matched-panel repair changes the protocol conclusion for
M5/Rohlik/Favorita.  On the same 100 top-volume series and aggregate-\WAPE{}
metric used for Table~\ref{tab:sourceb-paired}, direct \LGBM{} is slightly
worse than recursive on M5 at $h=7$ (+0.4\% error) and $h=14$ (+1.1\%),
but better at $h=28$ (-2.1\%).  On Rohlik, direct is consistently worse
than recursive by about 3.9--4.3\%.  On Favorita, recursive is lower at
$h=7$ (+4.6\% direct error) and $h=14$ (+2.2\%), while direct is lower at
$h=28$ (-4.9\%).  The horizon-scaled direct variant is identical to direct
at $h=7$ and worse at $h=14,28$ in the matched repair, so the earlier
unexplained \texttt{direct\_scaled} $h=7$ anomaly is resolved in the
repaired panel.

Three key findings:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Direction varies across datasets, workloads, and metric
  aggregation.}  The legacy per-series audit gives a strong M5 direct
  advantage, but the matched aggregate-\WAPE{} panel makes M5 close
  at short horizons.  Rohlik remains recursive-favourable in both views,
  while Favorita is recursive-favourable at short horizons and
  direct-favourable at $h=28$.  This is consistent with an intermittency
  hypothesis, but not sufficient to define a threshold or causal mechanism.

\item \textbf{The recursive horizon profile differs by panel.}
  In the matched aggregate-\WAPE{} panel, recursive \WAPE{} grows
  $+19\%$ from $h = 7$ to $h = 28$ on M5, while on Rohlik and Favorita
  the recursive horizon penalty is no worse than direct's.  This
  describes a panel-level pattern; the current three-dataset evidence
  cannot assign the difference to sparsity alone.

\item \textbf{Budget effects remain real.}  The matched repair resolves
  the earlier \texttt{direct\_scaled} $h=7$ inconsistency, because the
  scaled and unscaled direct runs are identical when both use 300 trees.
  At $h=14$ and $h=28$, the scaled direct variant is more expensive and
  does not improve aggregate \WAPE{} in this repaired panel.
\end{enumerate}

The M5 \WRMSSE{} rows are reported as internal rolling-origin diagnostics
only.  They are not directly comparable to the official M5 competition
winner because the competition used a 28-day forecasting task, a fixed
evaluation window, and different training/evaluation procedures.  sMAPE is
excluded from all primary
comparisons because Tweedie LightGBM's conditional-mean overshoot on
right-skewed demand triggers sMAPE's 200\% saturation, producing a
2--2.5$\times$ ranking flip vs Seasonal Naive on all three datasets
regardless of intermittency.

For the Source~A reanalysis (\S\ref{sec:meta-regression}), Source~B
direct-vs-recursive rows are not used in the primary contrast file.  They
remain a protocol hypothesis and a rerun target.

\subsection{Foundation Model Protocol}\label{sec:fm}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------

Foundation model rows come from two complementary data sources:
the PRISMA literature extraction (\S\ref{sec:fm_scope}--\S\ref{sec:fm_extraction})
and a local consumer-box sensitivity run on a MacBook via PyTorch
MPS (\S\ref{sec:fm_local}--\S\ref{sec:fm_results}).

\subsubsection{Scope and Two-Source Strategy}\label{sec:fm_scope}

FM rows come from two sources.  \textbf{Source~A} (primary) draws
on the PRISMA extraction (\S\ref{sec:prisma-results}): 9~FM papers plus fev-bench (B02) and
GIFT-Eval (B01) populate the FM grid entirely from published numbers.
\textbf{Source~B} (secondary) adds local consumer-box sensitivity runs on
a MacBook (M-series, 16\,GB, MPS/CPU backend).  The legacy Source~B sweep
contains five FMs (Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Moirai~2.0-Small, TiRex,
Chronos-2, TimesFM~2.5; 9--200\,M parameters) for cost-error provenance.
The matched-panel repair is narrower but paired: Chronos-Bolt-Tiny,
Chronos-2, Moirai~2.0-Small, TiRex, and TimesFM~2.5 on
M5, Rohlik, and Favorita, with the same series IDs and forecast
origins as the baseline panel.  The two sources are kept
separate: extraction rows carry
\texttt{paper\_id}~$\in$~\{A01\ldots A17, B01, B02\}, local runs
carry \texttt{LOCAL\_*}.  \S\ref{sec:metareg} reports the pooled
Source~A reanalysis; Source~B is reported as a separate local repair
analysis rather than pooled into Source~A.

\subsubsection{Literature-Extraction Protocol
(Source~A)}\label{sec:fm_extraction}

Extraction follows the PRISMA protocol of \S\ref{sec:prisma-results} and
records \texttt{paper\_id}, \texttt{dataset\_variant},
\texttt{metric\_name/value}, and a
\texttt{baseline\_quality\_tier}~$\in$~\{weak, standard, strong\}
per row.  M5 has the densest FM coverage (8 papers); Favorita has 7;
Rohlik~v2 has 3 (all from fev-bench).  Covariate asymmetry ---
most extracted FMs are univariate while LightGBM uses the full
covariate set --- is recorded as a moderator
(\texttt{covariate\_aware}) rather than treated as a filter.

\subsubsection{Local Consumer-Box Protocol
(Source~B)}\label{sec:fm_local}\label{sec:fm-consumer-box}

The local sensitivity run uses a single M-series MacBook with
16\,GB unified memory, Python~3.11 in a dedicated venv
(\texttt{\~{}/venvs/fm-local/}), and PyTorch~2.3 with the MPS
backend.  Inference is executed serially (no batching across series
beyond each model's own internal batch-size default) to match the
``retail practitioner on a laptop'' framing of the cost panel.

\subsubsection{Evaluation and Metrics}\label{sec:fm_metrics}\label{sec:fm-m5-metric}

Both sources use the \S\ref{sec:evalprotocol} metric definitions.
Primary metrics are \WAPE{} and MAE on all datasets, \WRMSSE{} on M5
only.  The local run reuses the same metric code path as the LightGBM
baselines.  For the matched-panel subset, aggregate \WAPE{} is
computed from prediction-level outputs on the same rows, and paired
bootstrap intervals resample the shared series panel.  The same bootstrap
draws are reused across all contrasts within a dataset, producing an
empirical log-ratio covariance matrix for the 45 completed FM-vs-best-baseline
contrasts.

\textbf{Log-ratio status.}  Source~A remains the only input to the

45 Source~B FM rows are retained in a descriptive contrast file for
audit.  The matched-panel Source~B contrasts are reported separately

aggregate \WAPE{} on 100-series panels rather than the as-reported Source~A
benchmark estimand.

\textbf{M5 caveat: per-series \WAPE{} + intermittent demand.}
The legacy M5 point-estimate gaps are large, but they are inflated by
the per-series \WAPE{} denominator on M5's long tail of near-zero-volume SKUs
(\S\ref{subsec:perseries-vs-aggregate}).  The $>$1 \WAPE{} values for
\texttt{lightgbm\_cov} (1.62--1.94) reflect this pathology, not a
labelling error; \texttt{lightgbm\_direct} (1.35--1.51) and even
\texttt{seasonal\_naive} (1.31--1.33) also exceed 1.  On Favorita
and Rohlik, where tail-sparsity is less extreme, gaps collapse
to 0.5--1\,pp --- so the M5-level gap is the ceiling, not the
central tendency.

A sanity check recasting \WAPE{} in aggregate form
($\sum|e|/\sum|y|$) deflates FM M5 scores by ${\sim}11\%$ in the
legacy rows.  The matched-panel results therefore use aggregate
\WAPE{} from shared predictions as the local Source~B estimand.

\textbf{Favorita is now the matched ``close call'' dataset.}
On the shared 100-series Favorita panel, \texttt{lightgbm\_cov} is the best
baseline at $h=7$ and $h=14$, while direct \LGBM{} is best at $h=28$.
Chronos-2 is clearly lower than the best baseline on all three horizons,
Moirai~2.0-Small is lower at $h=7$ and $h=14$ and close at $h=28$, and
TimesFM~2.5 is close to parity but slow.  By contrast, TimesFM~2.5 is lower
than the best baseline on the matched M5/Rohlik cells.  The result supports a
matched bake-off interpretation rather than a universal FM win.

\textbf{Cost and runtime footprint.}  The saved 72-row Source~B file sums
to 241.6 wall hours, \$3.18, and 4.63\,kg CO$_2$eq under the recorded
hardware assumptions.  These totals mix MacBook marginal electricity with
Azure job-level accounting and unequal workloads, so
\S\ref{sec:cost-accuracy} uses them only as scenario inputs.  Claims such
as ``16$\times$ cheaper per cell'' are not treated as general cost
results.

\textbf{Caveats.}  The LightGBM baselines use a fixed hyperparameter
grid with no dataset-specific tuning; the FMs are zero-shot; this
section is point-forecast only; the matched repair currently covers
100-series M5/Rohlik/Favorita for completed models including batched TiRex,
but not GPU throughput, larger workloads, or covariate-aware FM
configurations.

\subsubsection{Reproducibility}\label{sec:fm_repro}

Source~B runs are anchored by git commit SHA, HuggingFace revision
SHA, \texttt{pip freeze} snapshot, and machine fingerprint where
available.  The matched-panel runs include shared series manifests,
per-series predictions, run ledgers, and paired bootstrap contrasts for
M5, Rohlik, and Favorita, including the batched TiRex path required for the
100-series panels.  Future Source~B releases must extend the same structure
to GPU throughput, larger workloads, and covariate-aware FMs before
Source~B can enter a broader paired inference.  Moirai~2.0-Small carries a
non-commercial license (CC-BY-NC-4.0), flagged in
\S\ref{sec:framework}.

\subsubsection{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:fm_threats}\label{sec:table59}

Five threats are specific to the Source~B FM runs, detailed in the
risk-of-bias appendix.  In summary: (1) extraction selection bias --- only
papers that chose a retail task are represented; (2) Source~A vs
Source~B protocol drift --- as-reported vs our rolling-origin
protocol; (3) local run covers five models up to 200\,M params
--- the largest FMs ($>$200\,M) are Source~A only; (4)
covariate asymmetry --- univariate FMs vs covariate-aware
LightGBM; (5) top-30k Favorita cap propagates velocity bias.  The
largest residual risk is threat~(4): the within-Source~B FM-vs-baseline
comparison is univariate-FM vs covariate-aware-LightGBM.
