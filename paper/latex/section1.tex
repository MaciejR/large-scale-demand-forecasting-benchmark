\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The 2024--2026 wave of time-series foundation models ---
\citet{ansari2025chronos2}, TimesFM~2.5 \citep{das2025timesfm2},
Moirai~2.0 \citep{liu2025moirai2}, TiRex \citep{auer2025tirex}
--- has shifted the default assumption in short-term forecasting.
Where a retail team in 2022 would start with LightGBM and a
feature-engineering sprint, the 2026 playbook increasingly says: try
a zero-shot foundation model first, tune LightGBM only if the FM
disappoints. Each model paper reports favourable results on its own
evaluation suite --- GIFT-Eval, fev-bench, or a curated subset of
the M~competitions --- but no paper runs the same battery of models
under matched conditions on the same retail datasets and reports the
delta. The benchmarks multiply; the synthesis does not.

This paper asks: \textbf{when do foundation models actually pay off
for retail demand forecasting?} We operationalise ``pay off'' as an
observed, reproducible reduction in forecast error relative to a
clearly specified conventional baseline (gradient-boosted tree or
statistical model), evaluated under conditions that are as comparable
as the available benchmark evidence allows. By ``when'' we mean: on
which datasets, horizons, demand distributions, metrics, and hardware
budgets.

\subsection{Research questions}
\label{sec:research-questions}

We address four research questions across three complementary lenses
(accuracy, moderators, and cost):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1 (Accuracy):} Do zero-shot foundation models
    outperform conventional baselines on retail demand forecasting?
    (\S\ref{sec:rq1-accuracy})
  \item \textbf{RQ2 (Moderators):} Which characteristics ---
    metric choice, demand intermittency, model scale, forecast
    horizon --- moderate the FM-vs-baseline gap?
    (\S\ref{sec:moderator-results})
  \item \textbf{RQ3 (Cost):} What cost-error tradeoffs appear when
    consumer-hardware electricity, cloud-GPU list price, and
    CO\textsubscript{2} footprint are included?
    (\S\ref{sec:cost-accuracy})
  \item \textbf{RQ4 (Guidance):} Under what conditions should a
    retail forecasting team deploy a foundation model instead of
    investing in feature engineering for LightGBM?
    (\S\ref{sec:discussion})
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Approach}
\label{sec:approach}

This study combines a PRISMA-informed systematic review with
original gap-filling experiments and structured extraction from two
large forecasting benchmarks (fev-bench, GIFT-Eval).  Because the
paired statistical mass comes mainly from these benchmark suites, we
treat the pooled estimate as a benchmark-level synthesis and report
metric-specific and leave-one-suite-out analyses alongside it.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Source~A (literature + benchmark extraction).} We
    screen 150 identified records (2020--2026) from retained
    Google Scholar/web, Semantic Scholar, and arXiv searches, applying
    retail-domain inclusion criteria. Scopus and Web of Science target
    strings are reported as non-retained audit limitations rather than
    auditable exports. We supplement the extraction with per-model
    per-task results from fev-bench \citep{shchur2025fevbench}
    (20~retail tasks, 7~FMs vs LightGBM/CatBoost baselines) and
    GIFT-Eval \citep{aksu2024gift} (4~Sales-domain tasks, 7~FMs vs
    statistical baselines). The frozen extraction schema contains 802~rows
    across 9~retail datasets (\S\ref{sec:extraction-schema}).

  \item \textbf{Source~B (gap-filling experiments).} We run our own
    experiments under a unified evaluation protocol
    (\S\ref{sec:experimental-setup}): five foundation models
    (Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Moirai~2.0-Small, TiRex, Chronos-2,
    TimesFM~2.5; 9--200\,M parameters) on a consumer MacBook
    (Apple~Silicon MPS / CPU), and three baselines
    (lightgbm\_cov, lightgbm\_direct, seasonal\_naive) on Azure~ML.
    Source~B produces gap-filling rows with runtime, cost, and
    CO\textsubscript{2} metadata.  The integrity audit showed that
    the legacy rows were not consistently evaluated on the same series
    workload across all models.  The current repair reports a matched
    100-series M5/Rohlik/Favorita panel for Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Chronos-2,
    Moirai~2.0-Small, batched TiRex, TimesFM~2.5, and the baseline variants;
    the remaining legacy Source~B rows are treated as descriptive and
    as sensitivity checks rather than as primary inferential evidence.
\end{enumerate}

The primary reanalysis (\S\ref{sec:meta-regression}) uses Source~A
benchmark rows: for each (FM, task, metric) pair we compute
$\log(\text{FM metric}/\text{best conventional baseline metric})$.  The
primary file has $k = 498$ Source~A pairs.  We also generate heuristic
weighted \texttt{rma.mv} diagnostics for continuity, but the
$1/n_{\text{FM}}+1/n_{\text{baseline}}$ term is treated as a workload
proxy rather than as a validated sampling variance.  The full auditable
contrast table contains $k = 543$ rows after adding the legacy Source~B
export, but those 45 Source~B contrasts are reported separately because
they lack row-level paired prediction-error covariance.  The matched-panel
Source~B repair is reported separately from both files.

\subsection{Headline findings}
\label{sec:headline-findings}

Three findings motivate the sections that follow:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The observed FM advantage is benchmark-level and
    metric-dependent.}
    Across $k = 498$ Source~A benchmark pairs, SQL has the clearest
    directional advantage (median log-ratio $-0.229$; FMs lower in
    92\% of rows), while \WAPE{}/ND and \MASE{} are closer to the best
    baseline (median log-ratios $-0.047$ and $-0.036$).  The finding is
    therefore an observed benchmark-corpus pattern, not a universal claim.

  \item \textbf{Model scale is not a stable predictor in the current
    benchmark corpus.}  Source~B point estimates are clustered across
    several small and medium FMs, while Source~A does not yield a stable
    scale moderator.  Because Source~B is not fully paired, this is a
    hypothesis about the absence of a simple size--accuracy gradient,
    not evidence that one named checkpoint dominates another.
    This is consistent with
    \citet{shchur2025fevbench}'s finding that architecture may matter
    more than parameter count.

  \item \textbf{The LightGBM protocol gap varies across panels,
    metrics, and budgets.} \S\ref{sec:baseline-reliability} shows that
    the direct-vs-recursive multi-step gap changes with dataset,
    workload, metric aggregation, and compute budget.  Legacy
    per-series \WAPE{} makes M5 look strongly direct-favourable, while
    the matched aggregate-\WAPE{} panel makes M5 close at short horizons
    and mixed on Favorita.  Intermittency is a plausible stratification
    variable, but the evidence supports a protocol hypothesis to
    validate, not a hard threshold rule.  No retail-benchmark protocol
    recommendation that ignores demand distribution, metric choice, and
    compute budget should be treated as general.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{PRISMA-informed systematic review} of foundation
    models for retail demand forecasting (802 extraction-schema rows,
    9~datasets, 2020--2026).
  \item \textbf{Cross-benchmark extraction} from fev-bench and
    GIFT-Eval, yielding 498 Source~A model-level paired comparisons
    across 7~FMs and conventional baselines, plus 45 Source~B
    descriptive contrasts.
  \item \textbf{Model-level cross-benchmark reanalysis} ($k = 498$
    primary) showing metric-specific FM-vs-best-baseline log-ratio
    patterns, with heuristic weighted diagnostics kept separate from
    confirmatory inference.
  \item \textbf{Baseline reliability analysis} documenting the
    conditional direct-vs-recursive LightGBM gap across three
    datasets.
  \item \textbf{Practitioner validation checklist} mapping data
    characteristics to bake-off steps and cost-accuracy diagnostics,
    rather than deterministic model-family rules.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Paper structure}
\label{sec:paper-structure}

\S\ref{sec:background} surveys the forecasting taxonomy and existing
benchmarks. \S\ref{sec:methods} describes the PRISMA search
protocol, extraction schema, and gap-filling experimental design.
\S\ref{sec:prisma-results} reports the literature extraction results
(PRISMA flow, descriptive statistics). \S\ref{sec:experiments}
presents the gap-filling experiments:
\S\ref{sec:experimental-setup}--\ref{sec:perdataset}
experimental setup and per-dataset results,
\S\ref{sec:baseline-reliability} baseline reliability (direct vs
recursive), \S\ref{sec:fm-consumer-box} foundation model
consumer-box results. \S\ref{sec:meta-regression} reports the
cross-benchmark reanalysis: Source~A best-baseline log-ratios
(\S\ref{sec:rq1-accuracy}), moderator hypotheses
(\S\ref{sec:moderator-results}),
cost-error scatter diagnostics (\S\ref{sec:cost-accuracy}),
heterogeneity diagnostics (\S\ref{sec:heterogeneity}), and
preliminary findings (\S\ref{sec:rq1-accuracy}).
\S\ref{sec:discussion} translates the findings into a practitioner
decision framework. \S\ref{sec:limitations} discusses limitations
(single author, metric sensitivity, consumer-HW-only Source~B).
\S\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes with per-RQ answers and future
work.
