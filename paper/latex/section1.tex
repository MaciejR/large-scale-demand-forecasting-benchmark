\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The 2024--2026 wave of time-series foundation models ---
\citet{ansari2025chronos2}, TimesFM~2.5 \citep{das2025timesfm2},
Moirai~2.0 \citep{liu2025moirai2}, TiRex \citep{auer2025tirex}
--- has shifted the default assumption in short-term forecasting.
Where a retail team in 2022 would start with LightGBM and a
feature-engineering sprint, the 2026 playbook increasingly says: try
a zero-shot foundation model first, tune LightGBM only if the FM
disappoints. Each model paper reports favourable results on its own
evaluation suite --- GIFT-Eval, fev-bench, or a curated subset of
the M~competitions --- but no paper runs the same battery of models
under matched conditions on the same retail datasets and reports the
delta. The benchmarks multiply; the synthesis does not.

This paper asks: \textbf{when do foundation models actually pay off
for retail demand forecasting?} We operationalise ``pay off'' as an
observed, reproducible reduction in forecast error relative to a
clearly specified conventional baseline (gradient-boosted tree or
statistical model), evaluated under conditions that are as comparable
as the available benchmark evidence allows. By ``when'' we mean: on
which datasets, horizons, demand distributions, metrics, and hardware
budgets.

\subsection{Research Questions and Accounting Scope}
\label{sec:research-questions}

We address three research questions and one descriptive accounting scope:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RQ1 (Accuracy):} Do zero-shot foundation models
    outperform conventional baselines on retail demand forecasting?
    (\S\ref{sec:rq1-accuracy})
  \item \textbf{RQ2 (Moderators):} Which characteristics ---
    metric choice, demand intermittency, model scale, forecast
    horizon --- moderate the FM-vs-baseline gap?
    (\S\ref{sec:moderator-results})
  \item \textbf{RQ3 (Guidance):} Under what conditions should a
    retail forecasting team deploy a foundation model instead of
    investing in feature engineering for LightGBM?
    (\S\ref{sec:framework})
  \item \textbf{Cost accounting scope:} Source~B runtime, electricity,
    cloud-recosting, and CO\textsubscript{2} calculations are reported as
    descriptive deployment accounting, not as a universal cost-effectiveness
    test, because the Azure ledger is not fully reconciled
    (\S\ref{sec:cost-accuracy}; \ref{app:cost-envelope}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Approach}
\label{sec:approach}

This study is a matched-panel benchmark study supported by public-benchmark
extraction.  The strongest evidence comes from prediction-level comparisons
where the same series, origins, and actuals are shared across models.  The
structured search serves a narrower role: it documents why standalone papers do
not provide matched FM-vs-LightGBM retail comparisons and identifies the public
benchmark suites that can be re-extracted descriptively.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Source~A (structured search + benchmark extraction).} We
    use 150 identified records (2020--2026) from retained
    Google Scholar/web, Semantic Scholar, and arXiv searches, applying
    retail-domain inclusion criteria. Scopus and Web of Science target
    strings are reported as non-retained search limitations rather than
    exportable logs. We supplement the extraction with per-model
    per-task results from fev-bench \citep{shchur2025fevbench}
    (20~retail tasks, 7~FMs vs LightGBM/CatBoost baselines) and
    GIFT-Eval \citep{aksu2024gift} (4~Sales-domain tasks, 7~FMs vs
    statistical baselines). The frozen extraction schema contains 802~rows
    across 9~as-reported retail dataset names before normalisation
    (\S\ref{sec:extraction-schema}).  These rows are used for descriptive
    suite- and metric-level summaries, not for a confirmatory meta-analysis.

  \item \textbf{Source~B (gap-filling experiments).} We run our own
    experiments under a unified evaluation protocol
    (\S\ref{sec:experimental-setup}): five foundation models
    (Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Moirai~2.0-Small, TiRex, Chronos-2,
    TimesFM~2.5; 9--200\,M parameters) on a MacBook Air M3
    (16\,GB, MPS/CPU), and three baseline families
    (seasonal naive, recursive \LGBM{}, and direct \LGBM{}), expanded into
    seven baseline variants after fixed-budget, horizon-scaled, tuned, and
    richer-feature \LGBM{} checks.
    Source~B produces matched-panel rows with runtime, cost, and
    CO\textsubscript{2} metadata for a 100-series M5, Rohlik, and
    Favorita panel.  The final analysis reports this shared panel for
    Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Chronos-2, Moirai~2.0-Small, batched TiRex,
    TimesFM~2.5, and the baseline variants.  Earlier unmatched Source~B
    sweeps are retained only as provenance and data-integrity material in
    the appendices, not as primary evidence.
\end{enumerate}

For public-benchmark extraction, each (FM, task, metric) row is summarised as
$\ell=\log(M_{\mathrm{FM}}/M_{\mathrm{best\ baseline}})$.  The
primary descriptive file has $k = 498$ Source~A pairs from fev-bench and
GIFT-Eval.  Heuristic weighted diagnostics remain generated for pipeline
continuity, but the manuscript does not treat them as inferential evidence
because the $1/n_{\text{FM}}+1/n_{\text{baseline}}$ term is a workload proxy,
not a sampling variance.

\subsection{Headline findings}
\label{sec:headline-findings}

Four findings motivate the sections that follow:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Matched-panel local evidence favours several FMs against
    pragmatic LightGBM baselines.}
    Chronos-2 is below the best observed local baseline in all nine
    M5, Rohlik~v2, and Favorita cells, including M5 richer-feature and
    200-trial \LGBM{} stress tests.  The claim is local to 100-series panels
    and the tested feature sets; it is not a claim against competition-grade
    M5 feature engineering.

  \item \textbf{Prediction-level fev-bench reruns strengthen one public
    benchmark slice.}
    For official fev-bench retail windows, paired \WAPE{} covariance matrices
    can be generated for Seasonal Naive versus Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Chronos-2,
    TiRex, and TimesFM~2.5 across 20 tasks.  This is stronger evidence than
    the extracted leaderboard rows, but it is metric- and comparator-specific.

  \item \textbf{The broader public-benchmark pattern is metric-dependent.}
    Across $k = 498$ descriptive Source~A benchmark pairs, SQL has the clearest
    directional FM advantage (median log-ratio $-0.229$; FMs lower in 92\% of
    rows), while \WAPE{}/ND and \MASE{} are closer to the best baseline.  This
    motivates matched bake-offs rather than a universal FM ranking.

  \item \textbf{The LightGBM protocol gap varies across panels,
    metrics, and budgets.} \S\ref{sec:baseline-reliability} shows that
    the direct-vs-recursive multi-step gap changes with dataset,
    workload, metric aggregation, and compute budget.  The matched
    aggregate-\WAPE{} panel makes M5 close at short horizons and mixed
    on Favorita, while per-series \WAPE{} is much more sensitive to
    small denominators.  Intermittency is a plausible stratification
    variable, but the evidence supports a protocol hypothesis to
    validate, not a hard threshold rule.  No retail-benchmark protocol
    recommendation that ignores demand distribution, metric choice, and
    compute budget should be treated as general.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Matched-panel Source~B benchmark} covering five zero-shot FMs
    and seven pragmatic baseline variants on shared 100-series M5, Rohlik~v2,
    and Favorita panels, with paired series-bootstrap covariance.
  \item \textbf{Baseline stress tests} including an M5 stratified intermittent
    panel, richer-feature M5 \LGBM{}, and a 200-trial M5 LightGBM tuning
    sensitivity.
  \item \textbf{Prediction-level fev-bench \WAPE{} reruns} with empirical
    covariance matrices for 20 retail tasks.
  \item \textbf{Structured benchmark extraction} from fev-bench and
    GIFT-Eval, used descriptively to document metric-specific public
    benchmark patterns and the extractability gap in standalone papers.
  \item \textbf{Baseline reliability analysis} documenting the
    conditional direct-vs-recursive LightGBM gap across three
    datasets.
  \item \textbf{Practitioner validation checklist} mapping data
    characteristics to bake-off steps and deployment-accounting diagnostics,
    rather than deterministic model-family rules.
\end{enumerate}

\label{sec:paper-structure}

\S\ref{sec:background} surveys the forecasting taxonomy and existing
benchmarks. \S\ref{sec:methods} describes the structured search,
extraction schema, and gap-filling experimental design.
\S\ref{sec:prisma-results} summarises the supporting search and
benchmark-extraction rows. \S\ref{sec:experiments}
presents the matched-panel experiments:
\S\ref{sec:experimental-setup}--\ref{sec:perdataset}
experimental setup and per-dataset results,
\S\ref{sec:baseline-reliability} baseline reliability (direct vs
recursive), \S\ref{sec:fm-consumer-box} foundation model
consumer-box results. \S\ref{sec:meta-regression} reports the
supporting public-benchmark analysis: Source~A best-baseline log-ratios
(\S\ref{sec:rq1-accuracy}), moderator hypotheses
(\S\ref{sec:moderator-results}),
deployment-accounting diagnostics (\S\ref{sec:cost-accuracy}),
heterogeneity diagnostics (\S\ref{sec:heterogeneity}), and the
prediction-level fev-bench analysis (\S\ref{subsec:fev-prediction-level-repair}).
\S\ref{sec:framework} translates the findings into a practitioner
validation checklist. \S\ref{sec:discussion} discusses implications and
limitations
(single-reviewer screening, metric sensitivity, fixed-budget baselines, and
consumer-HW-only Source~B).
\S\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes with per-RQ answers and future
work.
