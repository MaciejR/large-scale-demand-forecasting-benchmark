\section{Decision Framework}
\label{sec:framework}

The meta-regression of \S\ref{sec:metareg} answers ``when do FMs pay off?'' in
statistical terms ($\Dhat$, CIs, moderator directions).  This section
translates those findings into a practitioner-oriented decision tool: given a
retail team's data characteristics and infrastructure constraints, which model
family should they evaluate first?

\subsection{The decision tree}
\label{subsec:decision-tree}

The framework is a three-question flowchart.  Each leaf recommends a starting
model family and flags the dominant risk.

\begin{alltt}\small
Q1: Does your dataset have >15\% zero-day fraction
    (intermittent / sparse demand)?
\(\mid\)
\(\mid\)-- YES \(\to\) Q2a: Do you need per-series point-forecast accuracy
\(\mid\)         (e.g., replenishment at SKU level)?
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)-- YES \(\to\) START WITH: LightGBM direct + covariates
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         WHY: On intermittent data, direct LGBM wins
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         by 17--22\% over recursive (\S5.3.6, Table~\ref{tab:t58}),
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         and per-series WAPE is unreliable as a metric
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         (\S5.4.5). Use WRMSSE or aggregate WAPE for
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         model selection. FMs post lower per-series
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         WAPE but this is a metric artefact, not a
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         real accuracy gain (\S6.7 M5 finding).
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         RISK: Per-series WAPE will mislead you into
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)         picking the FM. Validate on WRMSSE.
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)
\(\mid\)         \(\mid\)-- NO (aggregate-level forecast, e.g., category
\(\mid\)              planning) \(\to\) START WITH: FM zero-shot
\(\mid\)              WHY: At the aggregate level, FM and LGBM converge
\(\mid\)              (aggregate WAPE ratio \(\sim\)0.89, \S5.4.5). The FM
\(\mid\)              requires no feature engineering, saving 2--4 weeks
\(\mid\)              of data pipeline work.
\(\mid\)              RISK: No covariate channel in most FMs --- if
\(\mid\)              promotions drive >20\% of variance, LGBM wins.
\(\mid\)
\(\mid\)-- NO (smooth / continuous demand, <15\% zero-day) \(\to\)
    Q2b: Do you have rich covariates (promotions, price,
         calendar events) AND engineering capacity to build
         a feature pipeline?
         \(\mid\)
         \(\mid\)-- YES \(\to\) START WITH: LightGBM recursive + covariates
         \(\mid\)         WHY: On Favorita (covariate-rich, smooth
         \(\mid\)         demand), lightgbm\_cov matches Chronos-Bolt-
         \(\mid\)         Tiny within 0.3 pp WAPE at h=7 and beats it
         \(\mid\)         at h=14,28 (\S5.4.5). Recursive outperforms
         \(\mid\)         fixed-budget direct by 4--10\% on smooth
         \(\mid\)         demand; horizon-scaled direct narrows the
         \(\mid\)         gap but raises training cost (\S5.3.6).
         \(\mid\)         The FM adds no accuracy and costs more in
         \(\mid\)         inference latency at scale.
         \(\mid\)         RISK: Feature engineering is the bottleneck,
         \(\mid\)         not model selection. Budget 60\% of effort on
         \(\mid\)         features, 20\% on tuning, 20\% on evaluation.
         \(\mid\)
         \(\mid\)-- NO (limited covariates or no feature engineering
              capacity) \(\to\) Q3: Is wall-time latency critical
              (e.g., <5 min for 10K series)?
              \(\mid\)
              \(\mid\)-- YES \(\to\) START WITH: Chronos-Bolt-Tiny
              \(\mid\)         WHY: 9M params, \(\sim\)0.6s per series on
              \(\mid\)         consumer CPU, competitive with LGBM
              \(\mid\)         on Rohlik within 4--8 pp WAPE (\S5.4.7,
              \(\mid\)         Table~\ref{tab:t59}). No feature pipeline needed.
              \(\mid\)         RISK: On Rohlik h=28, seasonal\_naive
              \(\mid\)         overtakes lightgbm\_cov (0.412 vs 0.432)
              \(\mid\)         --- at long horizons on smooth data, even
              \(\mid\)         naive beats a poorly tuned tree.
              \(\mid\)
              \(\mid\)-- NO \(\to\) START WITH: TiRex
                        WHY: Beats Chronos-Bolt-Tiny on all 9
                        paired cells by 0.6--2.3 pp (\S5.4.5),
                        at \(\sim\)3.5\(\times\) the FLOPs. Worth the cost when
                        wall time is not binding and 1 pp WAPE
                        matters for the business case.
                        RISK: MPS xLSTM stall on Apple silicon
                        (\S5.4.9). Use TIREX\_FORCE\_CPU=1 or
                        deploy on CUDA.
\end{alltt}

\subsection{Cost thresholds}
\label{subsec:cost-thresholds}

The decision tree above is accuracy-first.  When cost is the binding
constraint, the \S\ref{sec:metareg} Pareto analysis adds a second dimension.

\paragraph{Consumer hardware (MacBook M-series MPS).}
The 45 FM Source~B cells ran in ${\sim}14$~h wall time at \$0.08 marginal
electricity (Polish grid) and 0.201~kg~CO$_2$.  Per 1,000 series per horizon,
FM inference costs ${\sim}$\$0.003 and emits ${\sim}$0.011~kg~CO$_2$.  This is
$16\times$ cheaper in \$ than the Azure E4DS\_V4 batch baseline (\$0.048 per
1,000 series) but $44\times$ higher in CO$_2$ per \$ (the Azure grid is
Swedish hydro, ${\sim}$0.02~kg~CO$_2$/kWh vs Poland at ${\sim}$0.7~kg~CO$_2$/kWh).

\paragraph{When the FM ``pays off'' in \$ terms:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item If the team already has a consumer MacBook and no cloud budget, FM
    inference is essentially free (marginal electricity only) and the accuracy
    delta on smooth-demand data is $<$1~pp \WAPE{}.  The FM pays off
    immediately.
  \item If the team has cloud infrastructure and a data engineering team, the
    FM saves ${\sim}$2--4 weeks of feature pipeline work but adds inference
    latency.  We express the run-count break-even as:
    \[
      N^\star =
      \frac{C_{\mathrm{feature\ pipeline}}}
           {C_{\mathrm{FM,run}} - C_{\mathrm{LGBM,run}}},
    \]
    defined only when the FM has a positive recurring cost premium.  Using
    the Source~B cost logs (\texttt{cost\_usd} in
    \texttt{benchmark/results/local\_fm\_sweep.csv}) and a conservative
    feature-pipeline saving of roughly 2--4 engineer-weeks, the illustrative
    threshold is around 50 production forecasting runs.  Below that range,
    saved engineering time dominates; above it, recurring run cost and latency
    dominate the decision.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{When the FM does NOT pay off:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item On intermittent-demand data where per-series accuracy matters and
    \WRMSSE{} is the evaluation metric: the FM posts 0.97 \WRMSSE{} vs
    competition-grade \LGBM{} at 0.52 on M5.  No cost argument overcomes a
    45~pp accuracy gap on the business-relevant metric.
  \item When the team has rich covariates that carry real signal (promotions,
    markdown schedules, weather): univariate FMs cannot access this information
    and the covariate-aware \LGBM{} baseline consumes it directly.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Recommendations by role}
\label{subsec:recommendations-role}

\paragraph{Data scientist / ML engineer} evaluating model families for a new
retail forecasting system:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Profile your demand distribution: compute zero-day fraction per series.
    If median $> 15$\%, you are in the intermittent regime and \LGBM{} direct
    is the default.
  \item Run Seasonal Naive as a sanity floor.  Any model that loses to it on a
    given (dataset, horizon) cell is broken.
  \item If smooth demand and no covariates: try Chronos-Bolt-Tiny zero-shot.
    If it lands within 2~pp \WAPE{} of your internal baseline, ship it --- the
    engineering savings justify the gap.
  \item If smooth demand and rich covariates: invest in LightGBM recursive
    with a proper feature pipeline.  The FM will not beat it and will not use
    your covariates.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Engineering manager} sizing infrastructure:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Consumer-HW FM inference (MacBook, no GPU) is viable for datasets up
    to ${\sim}$30K series with daily re-forecasting.  Above that, batch on
    cloud CPU (E4ds\_v4-class) is cheaper than renting GPUs, and \LGBM{} on
    the same CPU instances is faster per series.
  \item GPU allocation for FM inference is not justified on any of our three
    retail datasets --- the accuracy delta does not warrant the cost premium
    over CPU inference.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{VP / Director} making a build-vs-buy decision:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The FM ``pays off'' when your team does not have the bandwidth to build
    and maintain a covariate feature pipeline.  The accuracy sacrifice on
    smooth-demand data is $<$1~pp \WAPE{} (Table~\ref{tab:t59}, Favorita row) --- likely
    within the noise floor of most business KPIs.
  \item The FM does NOT pay off when you already have a production \LGBM{}
    pipeline with promotions and calendar features: swapping to an FM loses the
    covariate signal with no accuracy gain.
\end{itemize}
