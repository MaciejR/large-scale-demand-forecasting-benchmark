\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}\label{sec:methods}

This section describes the three-phase protocol: search and
extraction (Source~A), gap-filling experiments (Source~B), and the
Source~A benchmark analysis.

\subsection{Structured search protocol}
\label{subsec:search}

The search is used to document the retail benchmark evidence gap and to
identify extractable FM-vs-baseline results.  It is a single-author structured
search, not a registered systematic review: there is no independent duplicate
screening or inter-rater reliability estimate.  \ref{app:search}
reports a structured-search flow diagram for transparency, and the full protocol is
committed at \texttt{analysis/prisma\_search\_protocol.md}; the main text
summarises only the search parameters needed to interpret the benchmark
extraction.

\noindent\textbf{Search sources.}
The retained auditable log covers Google Scholar/web searches, Semantic
Scholar API, and arXiv (cs.LG, stat.ML).  Scopus and Web of Science were
target sources, but their export logs were not retained in the final
machine-readable search record; this is reported as a structured-search
limitation in \ref{app:search}.

\noindent\textbf{Search query.}
We combine three concept blocks with AND:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Foundation model identifiers:} ``foundation model'',
    ``pretrained model'', ``zero-shot forecasting'', Chronos, TimesFM,
    Moirai, TimeGPT, Lag-Llama, TiRex, TabPFN.
  \item \emph{Demand forecasting:} ``demand forecasting'', ``retail
    forecasting'', ``time series forecasting'', ``sales forecasting''.
  \item \emph{Benchmark anchors:} ``benchmark'', ``comparison'',
    ``evaluation'', M5, GIFT-Eval, fev-bench.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Date range.}
2020-01-01 to 2026-04-12.  The lower bound captures the M5 competition
and pre-FM baselines; the upper bound is the extraction lock date.

\noindent\textbf{Inclusion criteria.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Empirical evaluation of at least one foundation model on demand
    or retail forecasting.
  \item Reports quantitative accuracy metrics (\WAPE{}, \MASE{}, MAE,
    sMAPE, CRPS, \WRMSSE{}, or win-rate/skill-score equivalents).
  \item Uses at least one of M5, Favorita, Rohlik~v2, GIFT-Eval
    (retail tasks), fev-bench (retail tasks), or Walmart.
  \item Peer-reviewed or published preprint with reproducible
    methodology.
  \item English language.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Exclusion criteria.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item No quantitative results (position papers, surveys without new
    data).
  \item Only financial, stock, or energy forecasting with no retail
    overlap.
  \item FM paper without forecasting evaluation (architecture-only).
  \item Duplicate results --- keep most recent version.
  \item Non-time-series forecasting (causal inference only).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Row-level IDs and analysis clusters.}
Each extracted result is a separate row with a unique
\texttt{paper\_id}.  A paper reporting Chronos and LightGBM on M5
contributes at least two rows (\texttt{A01\_chronos\_indomain},
\texttt{A01\_chronos\_zeroshot}, etc.).  The current analysis therefore
does not treat \texttt{paper\_id} as a paper-level dependency cluster.
The R pipeline derives separate identifiers:
\texttt{study\_id} for the publication or Source~B experiment source,
\texttt{suite\_id} for the benchmark suite (fev-bench, GIFT-Eval,
Source~B, or other), and \texttt{task\_id} for the benchmark
task/dataset/frequency/horizon cell.

\noindent\textbf{Category prefixes.}
\texttt{A} = FM papers (17 papers, 58 rows),
\texttt{B} = benchmark papers and per-task benchmark re-extraction
(657 rows),
\texttt{C} = retail ML/DL papers (8 papers, 31 rows),
\texttt{D} = cross-domain papers (3 papers, 3 rows),
\texttt{F} = fev-bench entries (1 paper, 8 rows),
\texttt{OWN\_*} = Source~B baseline rows retained for provenance.
Source~B rows are not counted as included literature records.

\noindent\textbf{Normalisation.}
Raw \texttt{dataset} values are collapsed to five canonical names
(M5, Favorita, Rohlik, fev-bench, Walmart) by the R function
\texttt{normalize\_dataset()} in \path{analysis/meta_regression.R}.
Raw \texttt{model\_family} values are collapsed to four analysis
families (FM, ML\_TREE, STATS, NN) by \texttt{normalize\_family()}.
Rows that do not map to a canonical dataset or family are excluded
from the primary reanalysis but retained in the schema for
provenance.

\subsection{Gap-filling experiments (Source~B)}
\label{subsec:gapfilling}

Source~A has two structural gaps: (1)~no FM paper reports per-series
\WAPE{} on M5, Favorita, or Rohlik under a rolling-origin protocol
matching \S\ref{sec:evalprotocol} --- the FM literature evaluates on benchmark suites
(GIFT-Eval, fev-bench), not on individual retail datasets.
(2)~No paper reports both FM and ML\_TREE on the same retail dataset
under matched conditions with a shared \texttt{paper\_id}, so
within-paper pairing (the original within-paper design) is
structurally empty on Source~A.

Source~B fills these gaps with matched-panel reruns for M5, Rohlik, and
Favorita.  The runner
\texttt{benchmark/code/experiments/run\_source\_b\_paired\_panel.py}
selects the same 100 top-volume series per dataset, uses the same
rolling-origin windows for every model, writes prediction-level parquet
files keyed by series, origin date, target date, and forecast step, and
records a machine-readable run ledger.  Completed matched-panel runs cover
Seasonal Naive, recursive \LGBM{}, tuned recursive \LGBM{}, direct
\LGBM{}, horizon-scaled direct \LGBM{}, tuned direct \LGBM{},
Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Chronos-2, Moirai~2.0-Small, batched TiRex, and
TimesFM~2.5 on all three datasets at $h\in\{7,14,28\}$.  TiRex uses a CPU
batched path on the MacBook because the MPS xLSTM fallback is slower for this
workload.

For M5, we also run a stratified-panel sensitivity check.  Instead of selecting
the top 100 volume series, the panel samples across bins of total volume and
zero fraction.  This produces a much more intermittent 100-series sample and
tests whether the main Chronos-2 result is driven by high-volume SKUs.

An unmatched local sweep is retained only as provenance and cost-accounting
material.  It used related metric definitions and rolling-origin settings, but
the saved cells did not share a common series workload across models, so it is
not used for paired Source~B inference.

\subsection{Reanalysis specification}
\label{subsec:metaspec}

The primary estimand is the model-level paired log-ratio:

\begin{quote}
For each FM row $i$ and baseline summary that share the same
(\texttt{task\_id}, dataset, metric) cell, compute
$\ell_i = \log\{\text{metric}(\text{FM}_i) /
\text{metric}(\text{best conventional baseline})\}$.
\end{quote}

\noindent The primary Source~A model-level pairing produces $k = 498$
observations across fev-bench and GIFT-Eval.  The older Source~B export can be
appended for provenance, but those rows are descriptive because they do not
share a common workload.  The matched-panel Source~B analysis is reported
separately as a prediction-level paired analysis on FM-vs-best-baseline
aggregate-\WAPE{} contrasts with shared-bootstrap log-ratio covariance
matrices.  \ref{app:sensitivity} also reports a coarser bucket-level analysis
for comparison.

\noindent\textbf{Notation convention.}
Throughout this paper, $\hat{\ell}$ denotes a reported log-ratio summary of
\(\log(\text{FM metric}/\text{baseline metric})\).  \textbf{Negative
$\hat{\ell}$ means FM is better}; \textbf{positive $\hat{\ell}$ means
baseline is better}.  The older raw difference $\Dhat$ is retained only
in provenance outputs and descriptive columns.

The exploratory weighted diagnostic model is:

\begin{alltt}
rma.mv(yi = log_ratio, V = vi,
       random = list(~ 1 | suite_id / task_id,
                     ~ 1 | model_name),
       data = delta_model,
       test = "t", dfs = "contain",
       method = "REML")
\end{alltt}

\noindent\textbf{Random effects.}
\texttt{\~{} 1 | suite\_id / task\_id} nests tasks within source
suites.  A crossed \texttt{\~{} 1 | model\_name} random effect
accounts for non-independence when the same FM appears across
multiple tasks and datasets.  The suite level captures the strongest
dependence induced by benchmark-suite protocols; the task level
accounts for repeated models and metrics within a benchmark task.

\noindent\textbf{Variance.}
For Source~A pairs, $v_i = 1/n_{\mathrm{FM}} + 1/n_{\mathrm{baseline}}$ where
$n_\text{series}$ is the number of series in the evaluation task.
For the older Source~B export, the same proxy is computed with the recorded
\texttt{n\_series} value for provenance only.  The proxy is not a sampling
variance for a paired log-ratio: it does not capture metric dispersion,
shared baselines, covariance between FM and baseline errors, or
within-series autocorrelation.  This is flagged as a limitation in
\S\ref{subsec:limitations} and is why this paper does not present the weighted Source~A model as a
confirmatory meta-analysis.

\noindent\textbf{Small-sample and cluster-robust diagnostics.}
\texttt{test = "t", dfs = "contain"} uses containment degrees of
freedom in \texttt{metafor::rma.mv}; it is reported only as an exploratory
small-sample diagnostic.  We also report \texttt{clubSandwich::coef\_test}
CR2 diagnostics clustered by \texttt{task\_id}, with sensitivity checks
for \texttt{suite\_id} and \texttt{study\_id}.  Because the primary
model has a crossed \texttt{model\_name} random effect, the CR2 tables
are generated from cluster-compatible sensitivity fits and explicitly
labelled in the output files.

\noindent\textbf{Moderator diagnostics (exploratory).}
We test three moderators individually.  These analyses are exploratory
--- no multiplicity adjustment is applied, and results are
hypothesis-generating rather than confirmatory:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{mods = \~{} dataset\_norm} --- tests whether the
    FM-vs-ML\_TREE gap varies by dataset (H1 proxy for intermittency,
    since M5 is the only high-intermittency dataset in the pool).
  \item \texttt{mods = \~{} horizon\_bucket} --- tests whether longer
    horizons widen or narrow the gap.
  \item \texttt{mods = \~{} has\_covariates} --- tests whether
    covariate-rich ML\_TREE baselines reduce FM advantage (H2).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Sensitivity analyses.}
We report the following robustness checks:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Excl-M5:} Drops all M5 pairs to test whether the
    primary result is driven by the M5 \WAPE{} denominator artefact.
  \item \textbf{Metric-only slices:} Refits the model on \WAPE{}-only,
    SQL-only, and \MASE{}-only pairs so the mixed-metric diagnostic is
    not the only summary target.
  \item \textbf{Source~B-only:} Restricts to Source~B pairs
    (\texttt{LOCAL\_MAC\_*}), testing whether the cross-paper pooling
    materially changes the descriptive direction.  This is not paired
    inference.
  \item \textbf{Source-A-only:} Restricts to Source~A pairs (fev-bench,
    GIFT-Eval), testing the FM--baseline gap using only published
    benchmark results.
  \item \textbf{Average baseline:} Replaces the primary best conventional
    baseline with the average conventional baseline in each cell.
  \item \textbf{Leave-one-suite-out:} Drops fev-bench and GIFT-Eval in
    turn to test whether a single benchmark suite drives the result.
  \item \textbf{Equal-weight:} Replaces the sampling-variance proxy
    with equal weights for all paired comparisons.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Implementation.}
All fits use \texttt{metafor} \citep{viechtbauer2010metafor}; the driver
script is \path{analysis/meta_regression.R}.  Generated forest plots,
intercept tables, and cell-level CSVs are committed to
\path{analysis/figures/}.
