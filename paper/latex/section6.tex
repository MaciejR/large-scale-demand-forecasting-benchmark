\section{Cross-Benchmark Reanalysis}
\label{sec:metaanalysis}\label{sec:meta-regression}\label{sec:metareg}

The primary Source~A evidence is a reanalysis of two benchmark suites:
fev-bench \citep{shchur2025fevbench} and GIFT-Eval
\citep{aksu2024gift}.  The stricter comparator is the default:
each foundation-model row is compared with the best available
conventional baseline in the same task and metric cell.  The earlier
average-baseline comparator is retained as a sensitivity artifact.

Source~B is excluded from this primary Source~A pool.  The legacy local
rows do not consistently share the same series workload, and paired
prediction-error covariances are not available.  The separate matched-panel
Source~B repair covers M5/Rohlik/Favorita
(Table~\ref{tab:sourceb-paired}), but that local 100-series aggregate-\WAPE{}
estimand is not pooled with as-reported Source~A benchmark rows.

\subsection{Effect Size And Weighting}
\label{subsec:pooling-strategy}

The unit of analysis is one comparison per
(foundation model, task, metric) tuple:

\begin{equation}
  \ell_i = \log\left(
    \frac{\text{metric}_{\text{FM}_i}}
         {\text{metric}_{\text{best baseline}}}
  \right).
  \label{eq:log-ratio}
\end{equation}

\noindent Lower metric values are better, so negative $\ell_i$ means the FM
has a lower recorded error or loss than the baseline.  Log-ratios are
computed only for positive metric values.  The raw difference
$\Delta_i = \text{metric}_{\text{FM}}-\text{metric}_{\text{baseline}}$ is
retained in the generated CSV files for auditability, but the log-ratio is
the reported scale.

The script also computes

\begin{equation}
  v_i = \frac{1}{n_{\text{FM}}} + \frac{1}{n_{\text{baseline}}}.
\end{equation}

\noindent In this repair this quantity is treated only as a workload-count
weighting proxy.  It is not the sampling variance of
$\log(\widehat M_{\text{FM}}/\widehat M_{\text{baseline}})$ because the
benchmark papers do not provide paired prediction-level errors, metric
variances, or the covariance induced by shared actuals and shared
baselines.  Consequently, REML, CR2, $Q$, and confidence-interval artifacts
generated by \texttt{analysis/meta\_regression.R} are exploratory diagnostics
only.  The main text reports descriptive suite- and metric-level summaries
instead of treating a pooled $p$-value as confirmatory evidence.

The primary Source~A file contains $k=498$ best-baseline log-ratios:
414 from fev-bench and 84 from GIFT-Eval.  The rows cover 24 task clusters,
2 suite clusters, 2 study clusters, 7 foundation models, and three metric
families.  The full generated contrast table contains 543 rows after adding
45 legacy descriptive Source~B rows; the matched-panel Source~B repair
is stored in a separate file,
\texttt{source\_b\_paired\_panel\_fm\_vs\_best\_baseline.csv}.

\subsection{Prediction-Level fev-bench WAPE Repair}
\label{subsec:fev-prediction-level-repair}

The strongest remaining objection to the extracted Source~A rows is that
the workload-count proxy $v_i$ is not a sampling variance.  We therefore
reran a metric-specific repair for the official fev-bench retail windows
where prediction-level artifacts can be generated locally.  Seasonal Naive,
Chronos-Bolt-Tiny, Chronos-2, TiRex, and TimesFM~2.5 were evaluated with
\texttt{fev.Task.iter\_windows()} on all 20 retail tasks, producing
179,258 forecasts per model.  For each FM we then joined the FM and
Seasonal Naive per-series/window \WAPE{} values and bootstrapped series
within task to estimate task-level log-ratios and their empirical covariance
matrices.

\subsection{Heuristic-Weight Diagnostics}
\label{subsec:sensitivity}\label{sec:heterogeneity}

For continuity with the earlier exploratory pipeline, the pipeline still fits a multilevel
\texttt{rma.mv} model using the workload-count proxy $v_i$.  The generated
best-baseline diagnostic has point estimate $\hat{\ell}=-0.225$ with only
two suite clusters; the average-baseline diagnostic is
$\hat{\ell}=-0.286$.  These numbers are useful as weighted summaries and
for regression testing the analysis pipeline.  They are not interpreted as
formal confidence intervals or hypothesis tests because $v_i$ is not a
validated sampling variance.

The sensitivity direction is stable: excluding M5 gives
$\hat{\ell}=-0.226$, equal weights give $\hat{\ell}=-0.215$, fev-bench alone
gives $\hat{\ell}=-0.091$, and GIFT-Eval alone gives
$\hat{\ell}=-0.379$.  The split confirms that the apparent aggregate
advantage is stronger in GIFT-Eval than in fev-bench.

\subsection{Removed Funnel Plot}
\label{subsec:forest-plots}

Full per-dataset forest plots remain generated artifacts in
\texttt{analysis/figures/figure\_6\_forest\_*.pdf}.  They are useful for
auditing task-level heterogeneity but are not interpreted as independent
confirmatory evidence.

The funnel plot and Egger-style regression were removed from the repaired manuscript and remain
absent.  Their
vertical axis would be based on $\sqrt{1/n_{\text{FM}}+1/n_{\text{baseline}}}$,
which is a workload-count proxy rather than a true standard error.  Such a
plot would mostly show recorded task-size bands, not publication bias,
small-study effects, or selective reporting.

\subsection{Moderator Interpretation}
\label{subsec:moderator-outcomes}\label{sec:moderator-results}

Moderator analyses are hypothesis-generating.  Metric choice is the most
visible row-level moderator.  Dataset, horizon, model scale, baseline
family, and covariate availability cannot be interpreted causally from two
benchmark suites and a descriptive local audit.  The defensible conclusion
is therefore deliberately narrow: FMs often have favourable recorded
benchmark-level log-ratios, especially on SQL, but rankings depend on the
metric, baseline, benchmark protocol, and demand characteristics.
