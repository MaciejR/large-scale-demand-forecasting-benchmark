Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please consider the manuscript “When Do Foundation Models Pay Off for Retail
Demand Forecasting? Evidence from Matched Panels and Public Benchmarks” as a
Research Article in the *International Journal of Forecasting*.

The manuscript addresses a practical forecasting-evaluation question: when do
zero-shot time-series foundation models improve retail demand forecasts over
credible conventional baselines? Its main contribution is a matched-panel,
rolling-origin comparison across M5, Rohlik v2, and Favorita, supported by
paired bootstrap covariance, explicit baseline-strength checks, and separate
prediction-level reruns of official retail benchmark windows. The results show
that conclusions depend materially on the error metric, baseline feature set,
multi-step protocol, workload matching, leakage risk, and demand
characteristics. This empirical and evaluation-oriented contribution fits the
journal's emphasis on improving forecasting practice and bridging methodology
with operational decision making.

A historical, longer technical-report snapshot of this project is publicly
archived on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21338004). The submitted article is a
substantially restructured benchmark study with repaired matched panels,
additional models and baselines, prediction-level covariance analyses, stronger
sensitivity checks, and narrower claims. The technical report has not been
peer reviewed and the manuscript is not under consideration by another
journal.

The submission includes an anonymized manuscript, a separate title page,
highlights, a declaration of interests, and an anonymized replication package.
The work involved no human participants or animals. The author declares no
competing interests and received no external funding for this study.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
